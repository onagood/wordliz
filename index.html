Wordliz

Every letter is on the table. The words are hidden. Slide the grid until they line up.

How to play

The idea

A 5×5 board holds five five-letter words, scrambled into the grid. Every tile belongs to one of them — nothing is spare and no letter is missing. You are not hunting for letters, you already have them all. The work is figuring out which words they make, and getting each one back into a row.

Moving

Drag a row sideways, or a column up and down. You can tap the arrows instead. Tiles that leave one edge come back at the other. A whole line moves at once, which is the entire difficulty: putting one word right pulls the others apart.

Reading the board

  • Green — this row is one of the hidden words. It banks itself after a moment.
  • Amber — exactly the letters of one of them, wrong order. Reorder this row, don't break it up.
  • Red — a real English word, but not one of the hidden ones. The guess is wrong and worth 1 point for ruling it out. Each word pays once.

Only hidden words can be banked. Other real words are information, not progress.

Every word you turn up — banked or ruled out — briefly shows its meaning under the board, and on the end card any word can be tapped for its definition. The little dictionary ships with the game; everyday words like pronouns go without one.

Clues

Each hidden word shows a few of its letters in place, the rest as dots. Tap a clue with a dashed border to buy one more letter for 30 points. Two letters always stay hidden, so no clue ever hands you the whole word.

Free

No clock — the timer only records how long you took. A banked row leaves the board and the grid shrinks. Finding a word opens one more letter in one of the words still hidden, or in two of them on 6×6.

When a single row is left, an empty row opens beneath it. Columns can then park a letter in a socket, so the last word can be reordered freely. Every board is solvable, so there is nothing to lose — the only score is your move count. Finishing pays 10, 15 or 25 points depending on board size.

Rush

Two letters of each word show, and never more. The clock starts after your first word, not before, because the first one is always the slowest. Time payouts scale with board size — 4×4 pays out less, 6×6 more.

Each word buys time, but the payout shrinks: 20s, 18s, 16s, 14s, 12s, 10s, then 9s, 8s and down to a floor of 3s. Every word pays a point per letter — 4, 5 or 6 depending on board size. A banked row is replaced at once — a new word's letters arrive scattered across different rows, never as a ready-made line. At zero the run ends, and you can spend 100 points once per run to buy 20 seconds more.

Board size

Free and Rush come in three sizes: 4×4 is four words of four letters, 5×5 is five of five, 6×6 is six of six. Longer words mean weaker clues — one letter out of four narrows things down far more than one out of six — so 6×6 hands out clues twice as fast and starts with an extra letter already revealed. Best scores are kept per size.

Daily

One board for everyone, once a day, always 5×5. Solve it and your streak grows; skip a day and it resets. This is the only board where comparing move counts is fair, since everybody plays the same shuffle.

Sharing a board

Every board is generated from a single number, so it fits in a short code like PS9WXB (4×4 and 6×6 boards carry a leading 4 or 6). The code and a link appear when a game ends. Open somebody's link and you land on their exact board — same words, same shuffle, same clues. Nothing is uploaded; the board is rebuilt from the number.
